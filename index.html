THE IDEA

A failure is feedback.
Touch makes it useful.

Vision alone can miss a stuck cap, a hidden obstacle, or an unbalanced grasp. Tactile feedback reveals what happens at contact.

TARO turns that feedback into a failure diagnosis, uses previous failures to decide how to recover, and switches back to the task when the recovery is complete.

01 / REPRESENT

Touch becomes text.

A lightweight CNN–TCN descriptor summarizes temporal tactile signals from both fingers into contact area, three-axis forces, and rotation, referenced to the left sensor.

02 / INTERPRET & PLAN

Local contact. Global meaning.

Visual observations and tactile text produce a failure diagnosis in a fixed scene frame. Up to five global failures inform the next recovery direction and magnitude.

03 / CLOSE THE LOOP

Know when to intervene.

Need initiates recovery; End assesses when the current recovery is complete. Assessments overlap physical execution so the robot can interrupt and resume actions.

Two histories, two roles. Global failure history informs the next recovery plan. Recent robot motion history helps End assess progress toward the current plan.

THE PAPER

Abstract

Paper coming soon

Tactile feedback is increasingly used to refine intended actions in contact-rich manipulation. However, unexpected disturbances in real-world environments can invalidate planned trajectories, requiring robots to understand contact failures and devise new recovery strategies.

We present TARO, a tactile-driven framework for online failure recovery. A lightweight temporal tactile descriptor discretizes continuous tactile sequences into textual contact descriptions expressed in the left finger sensor’s local coordinate frame. We leverage the fine-tuned vision-language backbone of π0.5 to interpret contact failures in a global reference frame by integrating visual observations with local tactile descriptions. It then reasons over the global failure history to infer recovery strategies.

Two binary heads, Need and End, run asynchronously during physical action execution to determine whether to initiate recovery and whether the recovery is complete, respectively. This enables autonomous transitions between execution and recovery and supports online multi-round recovery. Across four real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, TARO improves mean task success from 38.75% to 85.00% compared with a separately trained recovery baseline without tactile input, a gain of 46.25 percentage points.
